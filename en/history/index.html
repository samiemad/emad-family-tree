Family history

What published histories say about the Imad (al-Imad) family, with a link for every source. The tree itself comes from the family’s own records; where a source agrees with it or differs, the person’s page says so. (Person pages are in Arabic.)

Origins

Tannus al-Shidyaq writes that the Imad sheikhs “trace their descent to a man named Imad from the town of al-Amadiyya near Mosul”; they came to Mount Lebanon, lived in several villages and then the Arqub district, and finally settled in Barouk. — al-Shidyaq, scan 83

His chapter 16, “On the descent of the Imad Druze sheikhs and their history”, is very likely the source of this tree’s table of old generations: names, order and events match almost exactly. It confirms Kanj’s line: “Abu al-Nasr begot Nasir al-Din, and Nasir al-Din begot two sons, Kanj and …; and Kanj had sons.” (Kanj) — al-Shidyaq, scan 83

The family’s villages

In his description of Lebanon, al-Shidyaq says the Arqub district’s sheikhs are the Imads, that the Imad villages “have al-Barouk as their seat, where they live”, and that Kfarnabrakh also has “houses of the Imad sheikhs”. He calls Kamid al-Lawz in the Beqaa a village “belonging to the Imads”, and mentions Shamstar west of Baalbek, which they held by “old right”; when Emir Bashir III took it from them in 1841 they protested until it was given back. — al-Shidyaq, scan 17, scan 189, scan 244

Ibrahim al-Aswad writes that the Yazbaki faction “has been headed by the Imad family” since the days of Sheikh Abd al-Salam al-Imad, and that each faction had its own Sheikh al-Aql. In the Druze order of precedence, “after the emirs come the Jumblatti sheikhs, then the Imadi sheikhs, then the Nakads, the Talhuqs, the Abd al-Maliks and the Eids; these are the holders of the districts”. — Ibrahim al-Aswad, Dhakha’ir Lubnan (Baabda 1896), Hindawi ed. 2018, p. 85–88

17th century

  • 1633–1636: Ghadban son of Imad was killed at Khan Hasbaya on the day Emir Ali, son of Fakhr al-Din Ma’n, was killed; three of his brothers died without children. In a battle near Qabb Ilyas between Emir Ali Alam al-Din and Emir Mulhim Ma’n, Sheikh Sayyid Ahmad Abu Adhra took part with 400 men. (See Ghadban and Sayyid Ahmad.) — al-Shidyaq, scan 83

  • 1660: after the Ottoman campaign against the Ma’n family, sheikhs Sirhal Imad and Ali Alam al-Din were appointed to the tax farm of the Druze mountain, until Emir Ahmad Ma’n defeated his rivals in 1667. (Sirhal) — Harris pp. 109–111, Wikipedia

18th century

  • 1711, Battle of Ain Dara: “Sayyid Ahmad Imad” was among the Qaysi chiefs who fought with Emir Haydar Shihab; after the victory the Imad leaders were confirmed as tax-farm holders of the Arqub. (Sayyid Ahmad) — Harris p. 115, Abu Izzeddin p. 203, Wikipedia
  • From the 1740s: the Druze split into the Jumblatti and Yazbaki factions; the Imad clan led the Yazbakis, with the Talhuq and Abd al-Malik clans. — Harris p. 119, Wikipedia
  • Sheikh Abd al-Salam al-Imad: al-Shidyaq records his death in 1788 (Abu Shaqra: 1205 AH, i.e. 1790–91, as in the tree), “wise and so eloquent that his eloquence became proverbial”, and says a dispute between him and Sheikh Ali Jumblatt split the Druze into Jumblatti and Yazbaki. In 1763 he mediated between emirs Mansur and Ahmad Shihab; in 1772 Emir Yusuf fined him; with Sheikh Bashir Jumblatt he then engineered the fall of Emir Yusuf and the accession of Emir Bashir Shihab (1788). (Abd al-Salam) — Rustum vol. 1 p. 12, Abu Izzeddin p. 203, Harris p. 122
  • 1797: Emir Bashir Shihab, with the Jumblatts and the Imads, moved against the Abi Nakad family; Sheikh Bashir Jumblatt and the Imad sheikhs killed five Nakad sheikhs at Deir al-Qamar. — Rustum vol. 1 p. 13, Harris p. 132
  • 1799–1803: Emir Bashir turned against the Imads, “impoverishing them and driving them out to Wadi al-Taym, the Hauran, Acre and Egypt”. From 1800 al-Jazzar backed the Yazbakis, led by the Imads, in trying to install a rival emir; most Druze sheikhs condemned the Imads in 1803. Abu Shaqra adds that after crushing the Nakads, Emir Bashir “turned his eyes to the Arqub district”, took it from the Imads and gave it to the Abu Alwan family, “a decisive blow to the Imads’ power”; they withdrew to the Beqaa and raided from there (Yusuf Khattar Abu Shaqra, al-Harakat fi Lubnan ila Ahd al-Mutasarrifiyya, p. 7–8). — Rustum vol. 1 p. 14, Harris p. 132
  • Land in the Beqaa: when Sheikh Ali Jumblatt took the tax farm of the Beqaa he granted the Imads the villages of Jebjennine and Kamed. — Yusuf Khattar Abu Shaqra, al-Harakat fi Lubnan ila Ahd al-Mutasarrifiyya, p. 82
  • 1778: Sheikh Abd al-Salam Imad signed the Yazbaki document delegating the faction’s affairs to Sheikh Ismail Abi Hamza. (Abd al-Salam) — Yusuf Khattar Abu Shaqra, al-Harakat fi Lubnan ila Ahd al-Mutasarrifiyya, p. 88
  • 1801–1802: the Imads backed Emir Abbas Shihab, whom al-Jazzar had appointed in place of Emir Bashir; when al-Jazzar came round to Bashir in 1802 they wrote to him and sent money asking him to appoint emirs Abbas and Salman son of Sayyid Ahmad instead. — Yusuf Khattar Abu Shaqra, al-Harakat fi Lubnan ila Ahd al-Mutasarrifiyya, p. 198–199

19th century

  • 1808: Sheikh Faris al-Imad went to Egypt “for lack of livelihood”, followed by Sheikh Ali, who came back in 1819. (Faris, Ali) — Rustum vol. 1 p. 14
  • 1820: Sheikh Ali Imad, head of the Yazbakis, backed the peasants’ uprising (ammiya) against Emir Bashir’s taxes. — Harris p. 135
  • 1824–1825: Ali Imad allied with Sheikh Bashir Jumblatt against Emir Bashir; defeated, they fled to the Hauran. Ali was put to death on the orders of the governor of Damascus; Abdullah Pasha in Acre then executed Bashir Jumblatt and Sheikh Amin al-Imad, and the Imad sheikhs’ houses were destroyed. Abu Shaqra says Ali Imad was badly wounded at the battle of al-Simqaniyya, and that Emir Bashir took over the Imad family’s property for about twenty years (Yusuf Khattar Abu Shaqra, al-Harakat fi Lubnan ila Ahd al-Mutasarrifiyya, p. 13–14, 31). Bashir’s campaign drove many Druze to the Hauran. (Ali, Amin) — Rustum vol. 1 p. 15, Farah p. 10
  • 1832: Imad sheikhs joined the Ottoman army at Damascus against Ibrahim Pasha of Egypt. (Sayyid Ahmad) — Farah pp. 13–14
  • Egyptian rule (1832–1840): “Khattar al-Imad” and “Abd al-Salam al-Imad” were made beys, and in 1840 Muhammad Ali gave them the rank of miralay and posts in Lebanon. Abu Shaqra says “Mulhim Bey Imad” was promoted to bimbashi in Ibrahim Pasha’s army, and that “Sheikh Khattar Imad” was among the “guests of the state” who fought for the Ottomans, fled to Egypt after Nizip (1839) and were made beys by Muhammad Ali (Yusuf Khattar Abu Shaqra, al-Harakat fi Lubnan ila Ahd al-Mutasarrifiyya, p. 22, 32). This Khattar is very likely Ali’s son (Khattar): al-Shidyaq says he surrendered with his father in 1825 and came back from Egypt with Sheikh Abd al-Salam in 1840 (al-Shidyaq, scan 86). — Rustum vol. 1 p. 105, Rustum vol. 2 p. 101
  • 1838, the Druze revolt: Sheikh Nasir ad-Din al-Imad was one of the revolt’s three leaders with Husayn Jumblatt and Shibli al-Aryan; they defeated Emir Khalil, son of Emir Bashir, at Ain Janam (Yusuf Khattar Abu Shaqra, al-Harakat fi Lubnan ila Ahd al-Mutasarrifiyya, p. 18–20). He was killed at Wadi Bakka on 4 July 1838, with some 640 men. Al-Shidyaq names only one Nasir al-Din in the family, the son of Abu al-Nasr and father of Kanj (al-Shidyaq, scan 86); Dhoha magazine confirms he is the son of Abu al-Nasr, which makes Kanj’s birth year of 1769 doubtful. — Rustum vol. 2 p. 28, Wikipedia: 1838 Druze revolt (citing Kais Firro, A History of the Druzes, vol. 1, pp. 70–75)
  • 1842–1858, Khattar Bey son of Ali al-Imad: Omar Pasha made him general kethuda running all of Lebanon’s affairs (1842); Mustafa Pasha kept him; Shakib Efendi made him director of the northern and southern Arqub (1843). He was one of the Druze muqata’jis in 1858, and a rival of Sa’id Bey Jumblatt, who turned his relatives Sheikh Kanj and Mulhim Bey against him. (Khattar, Kanj, Mulhim) — Yusuf Khattar Abu Shaqra, al-Harakat fi Lubnan ila Ahd al-Mutasarrifiyya, p. 44–46, 66, 73–74, 91–92, 100
  • 1860: Khattar Bey Imad led the Druze at Dahr al-Baydar, where his son Ali was killed, and then in the attack on Zahle; Mulhim Bey Imad attacked Deir al-Qamar with 500 men from the southern Arqub (“the Imads’ Arqub”); Sa’id Bey Jumblatt sent Sheikh Kanj al-Imadi to Hasbaya. After the French landing Kanj, Mulhim and Khattar went to the Hauran. That Sheikh Kanj al-Imad was alive in 1860 casts further doubt on the dates 1769 and 1837 the tree gives for Kanj son of Nasir al-Din. (Ali) — Yusuf Khattar Abu Shaqra, al-Harakat fi Lubnan ila Ahd al-Mutasarrifiyya, p. 109–134
  • 1860–1864: Fuad Pasha detained “Sheikh As’ad Imad” with the Druze sheikhs in Beirut; “Sheikh As’ad and Qasim Abu Salman, the two Imadis” were then exiled to Belgrade with about seventy notables, and released four years later. They may be As’ad son of Amin and Qasim son of Salman. — Yusuf Khattar Abu Shaqra, al-Harakat fi Lubnan ila Ahd al-Mutasarrifiyya, p. 134, 145

19th and 20th centuries: from al-Barouk

  • Mustafa son of Kanj son of Nasir al-Din: finance director of the Chouf, then a gendarmerie major (bimbashi) from 1899; exiled to Anatolia in the First World War, later a member of the Syrian Congress in Damascus. Not yet in the tree. — Dhoha magazine, “al-Barouk” (2016)
  • Ta’an (1867–1933): emigrated to Argentina in 1899, president of the Red Crescent society there, represented the Arab National Party at the Syrian Congress in Geneva in 1921, and funded the Great Syrian Revolt. — family papers (author unknown); As-Safir, 15 March 2008, issue 10949 (Najib al-Baini)
  • Fayiz son of Husayn son of Hamad son of Jahjah: director of the southern Arqub in 1921, then qaimaqam of the Chouf, Batroun, Zgharta and Jezzine, and acting governor of North Lebanon in 1952. Not yet in the tree. — Dhoha magazine, “al-Barouk” (2016)
  • Qasim son of Tamer: lawyer, director-general at the Ministry of Information and then of Defence; assassinated in Tripoli in 1975 while governor of North Lebanon. — Dhoha magazine, “al-Barouk” (2016)
